# Proof & IP Harvester: final 88-second video script

**Runtime:** 88 seconds maximum target  
**Voice:** ElevenLabs River  
**Read:** Warm, grounded, conversational. Sound like a colleague showing useful work, not a product trailer. Read "IP" as "eye pee," "AI" as "ay eye," and "WorkIQ" as "work eye cue." Do not read the repository URL aloud.

## Final narration

> Most weeks leave useful work scattered across chats and files: an explanation, a decision, a diagram that could help again.
>
> Proof and IP Harvester is a standalone skill for organizing approved work signals into Customer Proof, what changed for a customer, and Repeatable IP, what someone else can reuse. Microsoft 365 and WorkIQ access come from the host.
>
> A real, user-supplied study diagram reached an approved note. This illustrative review packet shows the next step: add a sharing plan and draft copy, so someone knows what to do next.
>
> This handoff is illustrative. It points the owner to the source, separates evidence from forecasts, and proposes a reply. It makes review easier; it does not claim a Teams message was sent.
>
> Then a person decides. The owner approved this example for reuse. That approval does not prove every technical claim or grant permission to publish. Accuracy, reuse, and sharing stay separate decisions.
>
> The public repo includes the skill, templates, a setup helper, and package checks. It still needs a compatible host for connectors, storage, scheduling, and notifications. The helpers do not retrieve Microsoft 365 data.
>
> The payoff is illustrative. The intended benefit: less reconstruction, more approved work to reuse. Harvest once. Help more people.

## Voice and mix notes

- Generate one continuous River take if possible; use the paragraph breaks as natural beat boundaries.
- Aim for roughly 150 words per minute, with short pauses after “host,” “next,” “sent,” “decisions,” and “data.”
- Give “Harvest once. Help more people.” a clean, unhurried read; do not turn it into a trailer tagline.
- Let the picture lead during the real-note reveal. Avoid music swells under evidence caveats.
- Derive caption timing from the approved audio. Disclosure labels are burned-in screen text, not captions.
- If the generated take exceeds 88 seconds, increase delivery speed slightly before cutting caveat language. Never exceed 90 seconds after the final audio/video mux.

## Editorial lock

- The only real example is the reviewed approved-note screenshot.
- The handoff and closing payoff remain visibly illustrative.
- No measured time savings, verified Teams delivery, autonomous weekly discovery, technical correctness, or publication permission is claimed.
- Real repo footage demonstrates package contents only. Compatible host tools and permissions are still required.
